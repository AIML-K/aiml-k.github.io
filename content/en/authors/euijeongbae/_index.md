While teaching mathematics, I often noticed that knowing higher-level mathematics can change the way we approach simpler problems. 
This led me to a rather bold question: Would studying university-level mathematics also change the way I understand and explain mathematics? 
Knowing that determination alone wouldn't be enough to keep me studying on my own, I put myself in an environment where I would have no choice but to study,
and that is how I ended up in graduate school in mathematics.

As AI advanced at a remarkable pace, my curiosity began to shift. 
I was initially interested in what mathematical problems AI could solve and where it would fail. 
Now, I am far more curious about how far I can expand what I am capable of doing with AI. 
I have always loved making things, and AI currently opens up more possibilities for me than any other tool. 
I hope to keep learning and building things with AI as the technology evolves. 
They say that every place you wander becomes a little more your own; these days, exploring new paths with AI does not feel quite so daunting.

One area I am particularly interested in is mathematics education. 
I am exploring whether LLMs can go beyond solving problems to diagnose how a learner is thinking and provide feedback accordingly. 
I hope to understand the field well enough to find where I can contribute. 
What I build may be only a small piece, but I hope that such pieces will eventually help make good education accessible to more students.
